London's most famous killer has a name, but nobody knows who owned it.
1888, Whitechapel. Five women were murdered. Who did it?
Police received hundreds of letters; one was signed Jack the Ripper. Maybe the killer wrote it.
But most letters were hoaxes, and that famous one was probably written by a journalist.
Proven: the murders happened. Probable: one man killed all five. Unknown: who he was; a DNA claim on a shawl is disputed.
So the most famous part of the case may not be the killer's work, but a newspaper's.
