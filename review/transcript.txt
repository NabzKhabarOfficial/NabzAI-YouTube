Street tiles preached resurrection, yet their maker hid in plain asphalt.
Who embedded the cryptic Toynbee message across Philadelphia and other cities?
The linoleum-like plaques appeared from the 1980s, often reading: “Resurrect dead on planet Jupiter.”
Investigators connected clues to Philadelphia, James Morasco, and reclusive artist Sevy Verna, but no courtroom-level proof emerged.
Proven: hundreds of tiles exist across U.S. and South American cities. Probable: one Philadelphia artist began the original series. Unknown: full identity, motive, and every copycat.
The message remains public because its author made anonymity part of the artwork.
